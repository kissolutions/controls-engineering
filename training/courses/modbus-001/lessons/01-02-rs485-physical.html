RS-485 physical networks

RS-485 is a shared differential electrical interface. A valid Modbus configuration cannot compensate for a broken conductor, excessive common-mode voltage, or an unsuitable topology.

Walk a trunk in physical order

Document the gateway port, the cable route, first and last sensors, taps, junctions, power supplies and possible isolation boundaries. Slave IDs do not describe physical cable order. RS-485 sensor nodes do not relay or regenerate downstream messages.

The field sequence

  1. Measure sensor supply voltage at the near, middle, and far devices under normal load.
  2. Verify A/B polarity using both manufacturers' documentation; the labels themselves are inconsistent across suppliers.
  3. Confirm termination only at the two electrical ends of a supported linear bus.
  4. Check biasing/fail-safe receiver behavior, signal reference, shielding, grounding and galvanic isolation.
  5. Verify transceiver unit loads, cable specifications, stubs, topology and gateway-specific device limits independently.

What does the ohmmeter actually prove?

With the network properly de-energized and measurements made only where safe and appropriate, two isolated 120 Ω terminations in parallel have an equivalent resistance near 60 Ω. The reading can be influenced by connected circuitry and does not prove resistor placement or waveform integrity.

Safety: never resistance-test an energized trunk. Do not change grounds, disconnect active controls, or isolate sections without an approved operating plan. Restore proper termination/bias when temporarily shortening a test segment.
